Technical Sources

Working calculations · 5 November 2026

Public notes from a potato-cell battery thought experiment aimed at a Raspberry Pi 2. Zinc metal is the sacrificial fuel; potato tissue is mainly the electrolyte (salt bridge), not the main energy store. These figures are working maths from cited public sources—not a built or tested power system.

Loaded cell assumption

An optimistic simultaneous loaded measurement from a school Cu–Zn potato worksheet: about 1.0 V at 10 mA into a 100 Ω load (10 mW). That pair matches Ohm’s law (1.0 V / 0.010 A = 100 Ω). Weaker nail-and-penny cells deliver far less under load and would need many more cells.

Cell counts for 5 V

Using that optimistic cell, a rough pack of 5 series × 35 parallel = 175 cells targets about 5 V at 350 mA (1.75 W)—near a Pi 2’s typical bare-board active draw, not a 1.5–2 A PSU headroom budget. At 7.5–10 W the same optimistic cell implies on the order of 750–1,000 cells. Weaker setups need far more.

Faraday zinc budget (175-cell pack)

Reaction: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻. With M = 65.38 g/mol, n = 2, F ≈ 96485 C/mol (NIST/CODATA Faraday constant), the electrochemical equivalent is about 1.220 g/Ah at 100% current efficiency.

Each of the 175 cells carries 10 mA. Load current is 35 × 10 mA = 0.35 A, but every series anode in a string still oxidizes at that string current, so summed anode current is 175 × 0.01 = 1.75 A (a factor of Ns = 5).

Ideal zinc budgets are not established real-world runtime.

Practical duration for a Pi-scale potato pack was not measured. Voltage sag, drying, passivation, conversion losses, parasitic corrosion, and unusable metal can stop the system much earlier. Galvanized nails are a thin zinc coating (often milligrams), not grams of pure zinc. Published potato-cell demos (e.g. multi-hour LED loads) are not sustained ~10 mA/cell Pi-scale runs.
