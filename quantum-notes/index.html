3. Why a qubit lives on a sphere

See also the PDF Why a Qubit Lives on a Sphere and Bloch sphere diagrams.

Thread · 1 post · started Thu Oct 22, 2026

Thu Oct 22, 2026, 8:13 PM ADT · View on X →
A pure qubit state is completely described by two real parameters and lives on the surface of a sphere for the following reason. Any pure state of a single qubit can be written (up to a global phase) as \[ |\psi\rangle = \cos\frac{\theta}{2}\,|0\rangle + e^{i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\,|1\rangle, \] where \(\theta\in[0,\pi]\) and \(\phi\in[0,2\pi)\). The corresponding density operator is the projector \[ \rho = |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|. \] Expanding this in the Pauli basis gives \[ \rho = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(I + \mathbf{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\bigr), \] where the Bloch vector \(\mathbf{r}=(x,y,z)\) has components \begin{align} x &= \sin\theta\cos\phi,\ y &= \sin\theta\sin\phi,\ z &= \cos\theta. \end{align} These are exactly the Cartesian coordinates of a point on the unit sphere \(S^2\). Hence every pure state corresponds to a unique point on the Bloch sphere, and every point on the sphere corresponds to a unique pure state (again up to global phase). For mixed states the same expansion holds, but now \(\|\mathbf{r}\|\le 1\), so the state lives in the ball rather than only on the surface. Thank you, @grok.
